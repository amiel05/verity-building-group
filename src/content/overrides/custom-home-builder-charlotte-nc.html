Consider every detail

Planning priorities for a high-end custom home.

01

Design for daily life

Room count alone does not make a home personal. VBG asks about arrival, entertaining, quiet work, pets, aging in place, overnight guests, storage, indoor-outdoor connections, morning light, and the routines that should feel effortless.

02

Resolve complexity early

Estate-scale homes may involve structural spans, detailed millwork, specialty glazing, pools, elevators, advanced mechanical systems, lighting controls, audio/visual integration, and extensive landscaping. Early coordination allows architecture, engineering, and procurement to reinforce one another.

03

Build a complete investment picture

Construction is only part of project planning. Owners may also need to account for design fees, surveys, engineering, site preparation, utility work, permitting, financing, furnishings, technology, landscaping, contingency, and post-completion care.

04

Protect the character of the property

Charlotte infill lots, established tree canopies, and Lake Norman waterfront sites call for different responses. A site-specific design can preserve meaningful views and mature landscape while improving privacy, approach, and outdoor living.

Our approach

A deliberate path from first conversation to final handoff.

Regional luxury builders consistently emphasize the value of discovery, homesite evaluation, architectural collaboration, detailed preconstruction, disciplined construction management, and an organized closeout. Verity uses those same proven principles while tailoring the working rhythm to each client and project.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    VBG clarifies the household’s priorities, architectural direction, investment framework, desired timing, and definition of success.

  2. 02

    Homesite review

    Access, orientation, views, privacy, drainage, utilities, trees, setbacks, and neighborhood requirements are considered before the design hardens.

  3. 03

    Team alignment

    Builder, architect, interior designer, landscape designer, engineers, and specialty consultants coordinate responsibilities and decision timing.

  4. 04

    Preconstruction

    Plans, specifications, selections, pricing, permitting, procurement, and schedule assumptions are reconciled into a buildable roadmap.

  5. 05

    Construction

    Field leadership, quality reviews, trade coordination, progress communication, and documented decisions keep the work moving with purpose.

  6. 06

    Completion

    Final inspections, punch work, systems orientation, documentation, and a thoughtful walkthrough prepare the home for its next chapter.

Before you build

A clear start to
your custom home.

When should a custom-home builder join the team?

Ideally during homesite evaluation or early design. Early builder involvement can connect architectural ideas with site conditions, current construction inputs, sequencing, and procurement before revisions become expensive.

Can Verity build on a lot I already own?

Yes. VBG can begin by reviewing the available survey, plans, utility information, access, and known restrictions, then identify specialists needed to verify property-specific conditions.

Do you work with an owner’s architect and interior designer?

Yes. VBG values a collaborative team. If disciplines are not yet selected, VBG can discuss the types of professionals the project may require without limiting the owner’s choice.

How long does a luxury custom home take?

Timing varies materially with design complexity, approvals, site work, selections, and procurement. Regional high-end builders commonly describe a substantial design and preconstruction period followed by a year or more of construction. Verity develops a project-specific schedule after the scope is understood.

How are changes handled?

A strong process documents the requested change, price and schedule effects, approval, and revised information before affected work proceeds. The contract and project systems establish the exact procedure.

What happens after completion?

Closeout should include inspections, completion of punch items, systems orientation, key documentation, and warranty information. The specific coverage and follow-up process are defined in the project agreement.
